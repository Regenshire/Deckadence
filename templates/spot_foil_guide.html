Start here / One card at a time

Choose. Apply. Refine.

Choose Foil Mask for foil or Gloss for a clear coating. Automatic selection, touch-up tools, and cleanup edit the active layer. Each keeps its own selection and Undo history; Save stores both. Gloss effect, strength, and overlay color are under Preview finish in Gloss mode. Gloss previews a raised clear surface with edge highlights, shadows, and reflections that follow the card tilt. It stacks above Rainbow, Surge, and Chocobo foil, keeping their patterns visible underneath.

  1. Open the card’s spot foiling icon.The card appears on the left with a live Rainbow, Surge, or Chocobo foil preview. Use Maximize for more room; Restore returns to the smaller window. New cards start without a saved mask.
  2. Choose what to find.Use Bright areas, Strong colors, Edges and texture, Dark areas, or Everything. Subject and Background use local AI. Choose None to skip automatic selection and keep the current mask for manual editing. Choose Whole card for full-art layouts; Fixed art box uses the outlined rectangle.
  3. Choose Foiled or Opaque.Foiled reveals foil in matching areas. Opaque · foil the rest keeps them opaque and reveals foil elsewhere in the region. To make bright areas opaque, pair that option with Bright areas. Text and border treatment can override the result.
  4. Set Area selected and Foil strength.Bright areas at 35% targets the brightest 35% of all pixels, including the background. A card with a large black background may have most of its colorful artwork selected. Increase Area selected to include darker pixels. At 100%, every pixel is selected before text, frame, and border treatments. Foil strength controls coverage within those shapes: 50% gives half coverage. Equal colors and cleanup can change the final area.
  5. Choose text and border treatment, then Apply.These choices override the automatic selection. Follow automatic selection leaves it unchanged. For full-art lettering, use AI detected and check Preview text. Apply spot foiling uses all the settings above the button.
  6. Exclude dark or light colors if needed.Open Clean up → Exclude colors, choose Darker than or equal to patch or Lighter than or equal to patch, then set the patch color and click Apply exclusions. This removes foil from matching brightness values in the current mask, including text and borders. It is separate from automatic selection. Undo restores it. For a white underbase, no foil means retaining white ink.
  7. Touch up, then clean up.Paint adds foil; Erase removes it. Magic Wand selects connected colors; Match Color finds similar colors across the card. Open Clean up to invert foil, remove small dots, or adjust edges. Undo and Redo are beside the preview tabs. With Pan in Preview, hover to tilt or drag to move a zoomed card.
  8. Review coverage.Mask overlay shows foil in the color chosen under Preview finish; fainter color means lower coverage. All Overlays shows Foil Mask and Gloss together in their chosen colors, with overlapping colors blended. Tools still edit only the active layer. Use Mask only in the individual overlay view to hide the artwork and see unselected areas in black. These preview choices do not change the saved mask. Tool amount mirrors Foil strength. A 50% brush stroke adds half coverage; separate strokes build it up. Effect strength changes only the screen preview. Check partial coverage with a print proof.
  9. Save when it looks right.Save keeps the draft. Cancel or Escape discards it. To restore the regular finish, choose Remove spot foiling, then Save.

Uncertain parts can be missed and may need touch-ups. The frame model downloads 118 MB on first use.

Subject / Background

Install or update Card Finishing Studio in Plugin Manager. Subject targets the character, hair, and equipment. On Whole card, detected frame panels are separated from the subject; Text and Frame treatments still apply. Background selects the inverse. The subject model downloads 179 MB on first use, then runs locally. These modes use Foil strength, without an area percentage. Overlapping artwork may need touch-ups.

For curved, angled, or illustrated lettering, choose AI detected, then Detect text with AI. Preview text shows a cyan overlay. Letters only selects contrasting shapes inside each detected area. Shapes cut off by a detection boundary stay unchanged, avoiding straight-edged blocks in the foil mask. Whole text boxes deliberately includes backgrounds. Standard card frame uses fixed text positions.

Review difficult cards before applying. The detector can miss stylized lettering or select nearby artwork. Apply the suggested treatment, then correct it with selection and painting tools. Apply replaces the draft selection; Undo restores it. Frame treatment runs first, then text, then the outer border. Apply color exclusions separately in Clean up.

Local text-region detection uses RapidOCR / PaddleOCR ↗.

Transparency uses opaque pixels for foil regardless of color. Brightness uses white for foil, black for no foil, and gray for partial coverage; transparent areas stay clear. Imported masks fit the card and retain detail up to 3024 × 4224 pixels. Import replaces the current mask; Undo restores it.

01 / The white-ink principle

The foil is already in the stock.

On reflective media, a white underbase covers the areas that should look opaque. Leave white ink out of the star, and the reflective surface can show through the color artwork there. Color ink remains a separate part of the print. Roland’s metallic-media explanation ↗

03 / Hand off the right file

A PDF layer is not an ink channel.

A named spot separation carries print intent. A PDF visibility layer organizes objects. An alpha channel carries transparency. The RIP—the software that prepares printer passes—must interpret the chosen format correctly.

  1. Save the masks on your cards.Only saved masks in the active image library are exported. Cards without a saved mask get no spot ink. For white ink across the whole card, open the card editor, choose Touch up → Clear selection, then Save for this card. An empty saved foil selection keeps the white plate; Remove spot foiling removes it.
  2. Open Print / Export → Settings.Enable Include saved spot-foiling ink layers · PDF and choose the Print workflow for this job. It applies to all cards; cards only store their foil masks. Choose Artwork + named spot layers for Roland. Ink and layer details lets you adjust the ink name and purpose for this export. Resolution, display color, and margins come from that workflow’s Plugin Manager preferences.
  3. Choose percentage or solid coverage.Preserve tool percentages keeps soft edges and partial coverage. For white underbase, 100% foil becomes 0% white, and 0% foil becomes 100% white before the ink coverage limit is applied. Solid areas only uses a 50% ink cutoff. Foil transfer, commercial foil, and toner always use solid masks.
  4. Include gloss when needed.Enable Gloss layer exports saved gloss as the named RDG_GLOSS ink. Leave it off to omit gloss. This works independently of the foil checkbox and requires Artwork + named spot layers. Gloss coverage uses its saved selection directly. The Gloss layer sits above artwork, below the optional White Inverse Layer.
  5. Choose the optional white overlay.Under Ink and layer details, Include White inverse Layer adds white wherever the exported spot mask is clear. Partial coverage is reversed too: 25% spot becomes 75% white. This is white artwork for color blocking, not another named printer ink. Top to bottom, the layers are White Inverse Layer → Art Layer → Spot Foil Layer. Without it, artwork sits above the spot layer.
  6. Check the PDF in the RIP.Named spot export contains true ink separations plus visibility layers for artwork and each ink. The spot plate uses your workflow’s display color (magenta by default); it is not the rainbow screen effect. Hide artwork and the optional white overlay in a layer-capable viewer to inspect the spot mask. Check the ink channel in the RIP and print a small proof on your actual stock.
  7. For a separate mask pass.Choose Black-only ink mask, then export the artwork with spot ink and decorative Foil Overlay off. Keep identical page, card, bleed, back-face, and registration settings. Use 100% print scale for both. Each export uses the chosen workflow’s ink channel.

The PDF keeps artwork and finishing separate. The original card pixels are retained; decorative screen foil is omitted during spot export. Masks follow card rotation, back-face placement, bleed, and rounded-corner clipping. Labels and registration marks remain in the artwork layer. New masks use the artwork’s available detail, up to 3024 × 4224 pixels. The editor shows the actual dimensions. Increasing export PPI alone cannot restore detail in an older saved mask.

04 / Tune the mask

What each setting controls

Selection defaults start new card edits. Cards save their foil masks. Print / Export chooses the workflow, ink purpose, spot name, PDF format, and coverage for the whole job. Workflow preferences supply resolution, display color, margins, and overprint behavior. The printer’s RIP controls ink passes, physical print order, curing, and media profiles.

Layer purpose
White underbase hides foil; foil / adhesive applies foil; varnish coats the selection. The custom workflow lets you choose among all three.
Spot channel name & overprint
Use the RIP’s exact spot swatch name. Artwork sits above the spot layer with overprint to retain the separate ink plate. PDF layer order does not determine the printer’s physical ink order.
Spot display color
In Plugin Manager → Card Finishing Studio → Configure workflows, choose a color or enter a six-digit hex value such as 838588 or #838588. Each workflow remembers its own color. Save workflows and export a new PDF; existing card masks do not need to be reapplied. This changes the spot swatch’s display color, not its ink name or coverage. Keep RDG_WHITE for Roland white ink. Workflows using the same ink name in one PDF must use the same display color.
Binary or grayscale
Binary uses an on/off region. Grayscale permits intermediate coverage. Transfer, commercial foil, and toner presets require a solid 100% mask.
Maximum coverage
Caps the intended ink / material channel value. It is not a calibrated foil-strength control or a replacement for the RIP’s ink limits.
Expand / shrink ink area
Positive millimeters expand deposition; negative values shrink it. Shrinking white can reduce white fringes. Check small details and registration with a sample.
Inset from card trim
Excludes deposited material near the cut edge. For white-underbase work, that can leave reflective stock exposed at the edge. Set it for the selected process.
Mask resolution
300, 600, or 1200 PPI sets the exported coverage mask resolution at final size. Automatic selection analyzes the available source detail, up to 3024 × 4224 pixels, without first shrinking it to a thumbnail. More pixels cannot recover details missing from the source. It is not the printer’s hardware DPI.
Automatic selection cleanup
Balanced smooths small edge irregularities and removes tiny disconnected dots and holes. Stronger produces broader, cleaner areas and can remove fine lettering or texture. Off preserves every selected detail. Cleanup runs only when you click Apply, before text and border overrides; it does not change later brush edits during PDF export.
Remove small pockets
Open Clean up to remove foil dots, fill clear holes, or do both on the current mask. Choose a preset or Custom for any whole number from 1 to 65,536 pixels. A 5 × 5 pocket contains 25 pixels; the same count covers less area on a higher-resolution mask. Click Apply pocket cleanup, review, then Save. Undo restores the previous mask. Diagonal pixels count as connected; pockets touching the card edge stay intact. Filled holes inherit the strongest foil coverage along their edge. Larger limits can remove small lettering and decorative details.
Initial selection & region
Choose Bright areas, Strong colors, Edges and texture, Dark areas, or Everything. Region limits the selection to the Standard art box or includes the Whole card. Set how much area to select in 1% steps, then Apply on a card.
Text & border treatment
Follow automatic selection, keep these areas opaque, or foil them. Use Whole card and AI detected text for full-art cards; Standard art box uses an approximate frame rectangle.
Text outline & smoothing
Select contrasting, light, or dark letters, or select whole text blocks. A positive outline widens that selection; a negative outline thins it. Smoothing softens its edges. Preview text shows the selection in cyan without switching your preview tab. Use Apply text only to update lettering while keeping the rest of the mask, or Apply spot foiling to rebuild the complete mask. Pixel widths scale with the card image, like the brush size. Ornate lettering may need touch-ups.
Color swatches & Sample
Touch up shows colors drawn from the card artwork. Choose a swatch or use Sample (I) to pick a color without painting. Apply color across card adds or removes foil wherever that color matches. Tolerance includes nearby shades; Tool amount controls coverage. The card remembers your last eight picked colors. Magic Wand (W) remains a connected selection; Match Color (C) selects matching pixels throughout the card when you click.
Shape and edges
Grow or shrink the current mask, smooth edges, or use Sharpen edges to tighten soft boundaries. Click Apply edge changes; Undo restores the previous mask. Sharpening preserves flat areas of partial coverage. Under Wand and Match Color, Selection edges adjusts only the next color selection.
Brush shape and softness
Choose Round or Square for Paint and Erase. Brush size sets the diameter or square width. Softness feathers the edges. Tool amount sets the maximum strength of one stroke; separate strokes can build up coverage. A hard brush keeps a crisp edge.
Invert foil
Under Clean up, Invert foil swaps foil and opaque areas across the current mask, including painted edits. Partial coverage is reversed too: 25% becomes 75%. It does not use Tool amount. Undo restores the original mask.
Review & reset
Review the card preview, then Save. Reset this workflow restores only that workflow’s draft defaults; Save workflows keeps them and Cancel leaves saved preferences unchanged.

05 / Deckadence → VersaWorks

A Roland handoff checklist

  1. Choose the process.For white ink on reflective stock, start with Roland UV / VersaWorks. Confirm the printer has white ink and a suitable media setup.
  2. Export the saved card masks.Enable Include saved spot-foiling ink layers · PDF and choose Artwork + named spot layers. Deckadence creates the RDG_WHITE separation. Use Preserve tool percentages for soft edges and partial white coverage.
  3. Inspect in Illustrator if needed.Check the RDG_WHITE spot fill and coverage mask using the steps below. If you save another PDF, preserve spot colors, transparency, and the original page dimensions.
  4. Inspect the RIP preview.Verify the white plate, color plate, scale, and registration. Use the supplied mask without an automatic full-white underbase filling its intentional openings.
  5. Print a small proof.Check white opacity, foil visibility, edge alignment, text, and fine details. Try solid coverage first; assess intermediate tints on the actual materials before a full run.

White-data preparation follows Roland’s Illustrator guide ↗. RIP controls depend on the installed printer and VersaWorks version.

The foil vanished, or the whole card is reflective

Check mask inversion and the RIP’s generated-underbase settings. A full white flood can cover intended foil openings; a missing or unrecognized white separation can leave the entire stock exposed.

The white plate is missing or prints as a color

Confirm that the file contains a true spot separation, its name matches the RIP swatch, and export did not convert it to CMYK. The color used to display a spot on screen is not the physical ink assignment.

How do I view the artwork and spot mask separately?

New exports place artwork above the spot layer, with the optional White Inverse Layer on top. In a PDF viewer with layer controls, hide artwork and the white inverse to view the spot mask in its configured display color. To view the original artwork, show artwork and hide both other layers. A black-only mask PDF omits artwork; choose Artwork + named spot layers to include it.

How do I check the spot fill in Illustrator?

Use a newly exported Artwork + named spot layers PDF. In Illustrator, expand its clipping group in Layers and select the filled path, rather than the clipping path or artwork image. Its Fill should be the RDG_WHITE spot swatch. The Transparency panel contains the coverage mask. For ink visibility, choose File → Document Color Mode → CMYK Color, open Window → Separations Preview, and enable Overprint Preview. Toggle RDG_WHITE to inspect the ink separately. A blank Fill for an image or clipping path does not identify the ink used elsewhere in the document.

The white plate looks blocky or speckled

For dots and holes in a mask you want to keep, use Remove small pockets, choose a maximum area, and click Apply pocket cleanup. Start small and increase gradually, or use Custom for one-pixel increments. This keeps larger shapes and current brush edits. Older automatic masks were generated from a small analysis image; to rebuild one, reopen the card with high-quality artwork, choose Balanced cleanup, and click Apply spot foiling to regenerate from the current artwork. Regeneration replaces previous touch-ups, so review and refine before saving; Undo is available in the editor. Save the card and export a new PDF. A bitmap still reveals pixels at extreme zoom. Intermediate ink percentages may also be screened into dots by the RIP; test those percentages on the intended stock.

Edges have a white halo or masks do not line up

Check identical page dimensions, rotation, scale, and placement before adjusting the ink-area shrink setting. Avoid applying the same choke in both the file and the RIP without accounting for their combined effect.
